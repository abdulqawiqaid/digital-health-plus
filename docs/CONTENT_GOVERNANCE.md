# Content Governance

## Editorial standards

- Use clear, professional language appropriate to the intended audience.
- Prefer current primary sources: official health authorities, peer-reviewed research, standards bodies, and original technical documentation.
- Verify dates, statistics, recommendations, and quoted material before publication.
- Cite sources close to the claims they support and include access or publication dates when useful.
- Clearly label opinion, commentary, preliminary evidence, and emerging technologies.
- Correct material errors transparently and record significant corrections.

## Medical and public-health content

- Educational articles must not present themselves as individualized diagnosis or treatment.
- Avoid unsupported dosing, treatment instructions, or claims of guaranteed outcomes.
- Explain important limitations, uncertainty, and the need to follow applicable local guidance.
- Do not publish patient case details unless there is a lawful basis, appropriate authorization, and effective de-identification. When in doubt, do not publish.

## AI and digital-health content

- Describe intended use, limitations, validation requirements, bias risks, privacy implications, and human oversight where relevant.
- Distinguish regulatory approval or clearance from research prototypes and general-purpose tools.
- Do not imply that an AI system replaces qualified clinical judgment.

## Review workflow

1. Define the audience, purpose, and scope.
2. Draft using authoritative and traceable references.
3. Check factual accuracy, dates, terminology, and links.
4. Review privacy, safety, copyright, and conflicts of interest.
5. Edit for clarity, accessibility, and mobile readability.
6. Record the review date and publish only after approval.
7. Revisit content when important guidance or evidence changes.

## Accessibility and presentation

- Use meaningful headings in logical order.
- Provide descriptive alternative text for informative images.
- Use descriptive link text and readable contrast.
- Ensure tables and embedded content remain usable on mobile devices.
- Avoid relying on color alone to convey meaning.

## Privacy and copyright

Never commit patient-identifiable information, private correspondence, passwords, API keys, access tokens, or confidential records. Use only media and text that the project has permission to publish.
