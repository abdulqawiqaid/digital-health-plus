# Scientific and Clinical Content Review Checklist

Apply this checklist to substantive medical, public-health, health-informatics, digital-health, and AI-in-healthcare publications. The depth of review should be proportionate to the potential impact of the claims.

## A. Scope and intended use

- [ ] The title, purpose, intended audience, and scope are explicit.
- [ ] The article distinguishes general education from individualized clinical advice.
- [ ] Clinical recommendations are not presented as a substitute for local protocols, professional judgment, or regulatory requirements.
- [ ] Conflicts of interest, sponsorship, and relevant affiliations are disclosed.

## B. Evidence and references

- [ ] Key claims are supported by traceable sources close to the relevant text.
- [ ] Primary sources are preferred: official health authorities, recognized standards bodies, original research, and systematic reviews.
- [ ] The source is appropriate to the claim, population, setting, and date.
- [ ] Publication dates, version numbers, access dates, and guideline status are checked where relevant.
- [ ] Statistics include their denominator, period, geography, and source.
- [ ] Evidence certainty and limitations are described; association is not misrepresented as causation.
- [ ] Conflicting evidence and important uncertainty are acknowledged.
- [ ] References resolve to the intended documents and are not fabricated or misattributed.

## C. Clinical safety

- [ ] Diagnosis, screening, prevention, and treatment claims are clinically coherent and current.
- [ ] Doses, contraindications, interactions, monitoring, and special-population considerations are included only when properly sourced and necessary.
- [ ] High-risk advice is reviewed by an appropriately qualified clinician.
- [ ] Emergency warning signs and appropriate escalation are included when relevant.
- [ ] No guaranteed outcome or unsupported superiority claim is made.
- [ ] The article avoids enabling unsafe self-treatment or delaying necessary care.

## D. Digital health and AI-specific review

- [ ] Intended use, target users, setting, and workflow are described.
- [ ] Validation evidence is distinguished from marketing claims, prototypes, and proof-of-concept studies.
- [ ] Performance measures, reference standard, population, and external validity are explained where available.
- [ ] Bias, representativeness, explainability, human oversight, failure modes, and automation bias are considered.
- [ ] Interoperability, data quality, cybersecurity, privacy, and lifecycle monitoring are addressed where relevant.
- [ ] Regulatory status is described precisely and is not inferred from research publication or vendor claims.

## E. Privacy, ethics, and legal considerations

- [ ] No identifiable patient or confidential institutional information is disclosed.
- [ ] Case examples are synthetic or appropriately authorized and de-identified.
- [ ] Copyright, permissions, image licenses, and attribution are checked.
- [ ] Ethical and regulatory statements are accurate for the stated jurisdiction and date.

## F. Editorial quality and accessibility

- [ ] Terminology, units, abbreviations, translations, and calculations are checked.
- [ ] Headings are hierarchical; links are descriptive; informative images have meaningful alternative text.
- [ ] Tables are readable on mobile and have clear labels/units.
- [ ] The article is understandable to its intended audience without overstating certainty.
- [ ] A suitable educational disclaimer and last-reviewed date are present.

## Review outcome

- [ ] Approved for publication.
- [ ] Approved after listed corrections.
- [ ] Returned for substantive revision.
- [ ] Not approved; publication must be held.

A review checklist supports a documented editorial process; it does not itself establish clinical validity, legal compliance, or regulatory approval.
